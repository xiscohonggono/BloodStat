# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

TypeScript + HTML, compiled to static files that open in a laptop browser with no server and no network (user's choice). Synthetic demo data is produced by a seeded TypeScript generator script.

## Users

- **Transfusion committee members and reviewers** at a Taiwanese hospital: once a month they review how red cells and plasma were used last month and adjudicate flagged transfusions against the patient chart.
- **Department heads** (main user): read a private monthly report on their department's transfusion practice compared with the hospital.
- **Data steward** (blood bank or quality office): checks and approves each monthly data load before any measure updates.
- **Committee chair**: owns and approves threshold rules.
- **Demo audience (current goal):** two professors, one in health information system development and one in biostatistics/bioinformatics, evaluating a pitch demo in early December 2026.

## Product Purpose

BloodStat is a retrospective patient blood management audit and feedback tool. Each month it matches every transfusion to the patient's most recent pre-transfusion lab result (Hb for red cells, INR for plasma), compares it with committee-approved thresholds, routes flagged cases to clinical review, and gives departments private feedback, so blood is not given to patients who do not need it. Success for the demo: the professors believe the data model, the measures and the evaluation design are sound.

## Positioning

Unlike point-of-order alert systems (e.g. Stanford's red-cell CDS), BloodStat is retrospective, needs no clinician data entry, keeps clinical rules under committee control, and treats plasma as a measurement and evidence gap (review prompts and dose feedback) rather than a "transfuse less" target. It is built around Taiwan's TW Core (FHIR R4) data standard and MOHW FHIR Box, and proposes a TW Core transfusion profile where the standard has none today.

## Operating Context

- Monthly batch around the 3rd working day; data load held until the steward approves.
- Lab, weight and encounter data map to TW Core profiles; transfusion events and blood products come from the blood bank system (TW Core v1.0.0 has no transfusion or BiologicallyDerivedProduct profile).
- Taiwan blood bags are 250 mL and 500 mL donation equivalents; dose calculations must use actual component volume.
- On-premise deployment under Taiwan's Personal Data Protection Act (product claim; not part of the demo).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Demo screens: data steward load approval, hospital overview trends, flagged-case review queue; later: department report with anonymous spread, threshold-change simulation, plasma review prompts, data-flow view.
- English first, with a Traditional Chinese toggle.
- All data is synthetic and must be labelled as such on screen.
- Undecided: final threshold source (AABB 2023 vs the guideline effective 1 April 2026).

## Brand Commitments

- Hospital shown as a fictional "Demo Medical Centre". No Tzu Chi name or logo in the UI.
- "Flag, don't blame": language never accuses individual clinicians; individual doctors are never named or ranked publicly.

## Evidence on Hand

- No real hospital data. Synthetic data only, generated by script.
- Concept docs and pitch deck (Google Slides "BloodStat").
- Do not fabricate outcome results, validation results, customers or hospital partnerships.

## Product Principles

1. Clinicians decide the rules; the software only applies them.
2. Flag, don't blame: every flag is a prompt for review, not a verdict.
3. Show data quality openly: missing labs, weights and unmatched records are reported, never hidden.
4. Plasma claims stay modest: label uncertain evidence as uncertain.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Projected in a room and read on a laptop: high contrast, readable at a distance, colour never the only carrier of meaning.
